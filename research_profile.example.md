# Research Profile

This is a template. `research_profile.md` (the file the tool actually reads) is
gitignored, so your real profile never leaves your machine — copy this file to create it:

```sh
cp research_profile.example.md research_profile.md
```

Everything in that file is read verbatim into the scoring prompt, so write it for a
reader who knows the field but not your project. Edit it whenever your focus shifts —
it is the single highest-leverage knob in this pipeline.

The content below is illustrative. Replace it with your actual ideas.

---

## What I'm working on

I'm interested in human-AI interaction: how people direct, correct, and build trust in
AI systems that act on their behalf, and what interface and interaction designs make
that control legible rather than illusory.

## Specific ideas I'm tracking

- **Steering and correction mid-task.** How users redirect an agent that is already
  working, and what feedback makes the agent's current state legible enough to steer.
- **Calibrated trust.** Whether users' confidence in a system tracks its actual
  reliability, and what interface signals move that calibration in either direction.
- **Delegation boundaries.** How people decide what to hand to an AI system versus keep,
  and how that boundary shifts with experience.
- **Evaluating interaction, not just output.** Study designs and metrics that measure the
  quality of the collaboration rather than the quality of a single generated artifact.

## What counts as relevant

A paper is relevant if its substance bears on these ideas — the mechanism it proposes,
the phenomenon it measures, or the study it runs — even when its title and framing come
from a different subfield. A systems paper that happens to include a well-designed user
study of how people override the system is relevant. A paper about interpretability that
is really about giving users an actionable handle on model behavior is relevant.

## What does not count

- Benchmark-only papers with no human in the loop, unless the benchmark is explicitly
  measuring an interaction property.
- Papers that mention "user" or "human" only in the motivation and never return to it.
- Pure capability or scaling results with no interaction, control, or trust dimension.
- Position papers with no new study, system, or data, unless the argument is unusually
  specific and directly reframes one of the ideas above.
